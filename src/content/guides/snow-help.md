## Start here: what the towns actually clear

Oakville, Burlington and Milton each run a paid program that clears the **windrow**: the heavy ridge of snow the plow leaves across the end of the driveway. Halton Hills does not have a program.

None of the town programs clear the **driveway itself, walkways, steps or porches**, and none clear the ridge left by a sidewalk plow. Most older adults who can't shovel need two kinds of help:

1. **The windrow**, from a town program if they qualify and registered in time.
2. **The driveway, walkway and steps**, from family, a neighbour, a community program, a volunteer or a private company.

## By town

### Oakville

<div class="program">

**Driveway Windrow Snow Clearing Program, Town of Oakville**

<span class="status">Registration closed</span>

- **Cost for 2026-2027:** $190.27 plus HST ($215 with tax), per driveway, for the whole season. The fee isn't refunded once your application is processed, however much or little it snows.
- **Who can register:** any Oakville resident. Early registration (October 1 to 15) was reserved for people 65 or older, people with a physical disability or medical condition, and homes with no able-bodied person under 65. General registration ran October 16 to November 7, capped at 1,100 driveways.
- **When it runs:** December 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027.
- **How it works:** windrow clearing starts only after more than 7.5 cm of snow and after the Town finishes plowing all roads. It can take up to 36 hours after the snow stops. The crew clears about 80 per cent of the windrow, not down to bare pavement, once per snowfall.
- **Not included:** the driveway itself, windrows left by sidewalk plows, private roads, unassumed roads and laneways. The Town says patterned or special driveway surfaces may be damaged, and it isn't responsible for driveway or curb damage.
- **Contact:** ServiceOakville, [905-845-6601](tel:+19058456601) or service@oakville.ca
- **Official page:** [oakville.ca, Driveway Windrow Snow Clearing](https://www.oakville.ca/transportation-roads/roads-sidewalks/snow-clearing/driveway-windrow-snow-clearing/)

</div>

### Burlington

<div class="program">

**Windrow Clearing Program, City of Burlington**

<span class="status status-open">Listed as open until November 21, 2026, or while spots remain</span>

- **Cost for 2026-2027:** $150 plus HST per driveway entrance, for the season. Not refunded, even if it doesn't snow.
- **Half price** if the resident is 65 or older; has a disability or physical limitation that prevents clearing the windrow and no able-bodied person aged 16 to 65 lives there; receives the Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS) or ODSP; or is enrolled in the City's Recreation Fee Assistance or Transit Low Income Pass program. You confirm eligibility yourself, and the City may ask for proof.
- **When it runs:** December 1 to March 31.
- **How it works:** service starts after more than 7.5 cm of snow. The City aims to plow residential roads within about 24 hours after the snow stops and clear windrows within about 8 hours after that. Windrows are cleared to a snow-packed surface, not bare pavement.
- **Not included:** the driveway itself and windrows left by sidewalk plows.
- **How to register:** online through a Live and Play account (Course Registration, then Other City Services, then Windrow Clearing Program), or in person:
  - Service Burlington, 426 Brant St., Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.
  - Burlington Seniors Centre, 2285 New St., Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.
  - Tansley Woods Community Centre, 1996 Itabashi Way, Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., and Saturday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.
- **Please call first.** Another public listing shows an earlier closing date, and the City doesn't accept late registrations.
- **Contact:** [905-335-7777](tel:+19053357777), toll-free 1-877-213-3609
- **Official page:** [burlington.ca, Windrow Clearing Program](https://www.burlington.ca/en/roads-parking-and-traffic/windrow-clearing-for-persons-with-disabilities.aspx)

## Driveways, walkways and steps (all four towns)

The town programs stop at the end of the driveway. These are the main options for everything else. Family Wayfinder doesn't vet, arrange or guarantee any of them, and we take no referral fees.

<div class="program">

**Links2Care Home Maintenance and Repair**

A referral service for older adults and adults with disabilities across Halton. Links2Care connects you with independent local providers for jobs inside and outside the home, including snow removal. Links2Care says its providers complete a Vulnerable Sector Screening and an interview before joining.

- **Cost:** you pay the provider. Links2Care's listing gives about $40 to $60 an hour, depending on the job.
- **Good to know:** one listing notes snow removal spaces are limited, so call early.
- **Contact:** [905-844-0252](tel:+19058440252), toll-free 1-866-844-0252, info@links2care.ca. No referral needed.
- **Official page:** [links2care.ca, Home Maintenance and Repair](https://www.links2care.ca/program/home-maintenance-referral/)

</div>

<div class="program">

**Snow Angels Canada (volunteers)**

A free, national, non-profit website. People who need help post a request or search for volunteers near their postal code, and volunteers offer to shovel.

- **Cost:** free.
- **Please read this part:** Snow Angels Canada says it doesn't manage or assign volunteers; it only connects neighbours. Volunteers aren't screened, help isn't guaranteed, and you arrange everything directly with the volunteer. Some families have an adult child or neighbour make the first contact and meet the volunteer.
- **How to use it:** register at [snowangelscanada.ca](https://snowangelscanada.ca/). Requests are made through the website only.

</div>

<div class="program">

**People you already know**

Often the most dependable help is close by: a neighbour who already clears their own driveway, a relative, or someone from a faith community or club. A clear arrangement helps everyone. Agree on what gets cleared, how soon after a snowfall and who to call if they're away. Offer to pay or trade a favour if that suits you both.

</div>

## Hiring a private snow removal service safely

### Questions to ask

- **What's included?** Driveway, windrow, walkway, front steps, porch, a path to the bins? Is salting included or extra?
- **When do you come?** At what snow depth do you start (for example 5 cm), and how many hours after the snow stops?
- **How many visits?** Do you come back during a long storm, or once at the end? Is there an extra charge after a very large snowfall?
- **Who does the work?** Your own crew or subcontractors? Do you carry liability insurance, and will you show proof?
- **What if something is damaged?** Lawn, curb, garage door, interlock?
- **Who do we call** if you don't show up, and how quickly will you answer?
- **References:** can you give us two nearby customers? Ask neighbours who they use, too.

### Get it in writing

Before you agree, get a written agreement or quote that shows the price, the dates of service, exactly what's cleared, the snow depth that triggers a visit, how soon they come, the payment schedule and how to cancel. Keep a copy where the whole family can find it.

### Paying

- For per-visit service, **pay after the work is done**, and get a receipt.
- For seasonal contracts, avoid paying the full season up front to a company you don't know. Paying in instalments protects you if they stop showing up.
- Pay by cheque, credit card or e-transfer so there's a record. Be wary of anyone who wants cash only.

### Be careful with door-to-door offers

Be cautious with anyone who knocks on the door offering a cheap seasonal deal, especially if they want payment right away or push for a decision that day. Ask for the offer in writing, then talk it over with family first.

In Ontario, if you sign a contract in your home, you generally have a **10-day cooling-off period** to cancel for any reason, starting the day you receive a written copy of the agreement. For questions or complaints, contact Consumer Protection Ontario at [1-800-889-9768](tel:+18008899768) or visit [ontario.ca/contracts](https://ontario.ca/contracts).

## Keeping an older adult safe through the winter

- **Salt, sand or ice melt by the door** before the first storm, in a small container that's easy to lift, with a scoop.
- **A check-in plan.** Agree who calls after every big snowfall and at what time, and who goes over if there's no answer. Share it with a neighbour.
- **A plan for when help doesn't show.** Have a backup name and number for the driveway, and stay inside until the path is clear.
- **Good lighting** on the steps and walkway, and a light left on near the door.
- **Boots with good grip.** Slip-on ice grips can help on icy walks.
- **Clear vents.** After a storm, make sure the furnace and dryer vents on the outside of the house aren't blocked by snow.
- **A few days of supplies** in case roads are bad: food, medications, flashlight and batteries, and a charged phone.
- **Shovelling is hard physical work.** If you're not sure it's safe for your parent to shovel, ask their doctor, and arrange help before the first snowfall.

### Who to call

- **Emergency:** 911
- **Roads and sidewalks:** your town's main line. Oakville 905-845-6601, Burlington 905-335-7777, Milton 905-878-7252, Halton Hills 905-873-2600.
- **Community services near you:** Ontario 211 (dial 2-1-1)
- **Regular check-in calls and other home supports:** Links2Care, [905-844-0252](tel:+19058440252). Their programs include phone calls to older adults who live alone.
