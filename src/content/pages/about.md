## Why I started Family Wayfinder

I've lived in Halton since 2014. In that time, my own family and my extended family have been through this more than once: someone we love suddenly needs more help, and a group of adult children has to work out what happens next.

Every time, it felt like starting from zero. Who do you call first? What does Ontario Health atHome actually do, and how long does it take? What's the difference between a retirement home and long-term care, and who pays for what? Who is allowed to make decisions? The answers exist, but they're spread across agencies, websites and phone lines, and you usually need them in a hurry, while also working, raising kids or living an hour away.

Looking back, what would have helped most wasn't more information. It was someone who knew the system, could sit down with us, separate what mattered now from what could wait, and hand us a clear list of next steps.

My career has been in consumer-facing marketing, where the job is understanding what people need and making complicated choices simpler. As I've thought about what I want the next chapter of my work to be, elder care is what I keep coming back to. It matters, it's personal, and too many families go through it alone.

So I started Family Wayfinder. I'm not a nurse or a social worker, and I don't pretend to be. I'm someone who has sat on your side of the table, has done the homework since, and keeps doing it. I'll help you understand your options, make full use of the free services, and leave you with a plan you can actually follow.

## Independent, and paid only by families

Family Wayfinder is paid only by the families it serves. We accept no commissions, referral fees or other payment from care providers, retirement homes, long-term care homes or vendors. When we mention an organization, it's for your information, not an endorsement.

## Alongside free services, not instead of them

Halton has good free navigation services, including Ontario Health atHome, Links2Care and the Connected Care Halton Navigation Line. We see them as partners. Part of every Roadmap is making sure you're using every free service your family is entitled to. If those are all you need, we'll tell you on the fit call.

## What we do and don't do

We help families understand their options, prepare for assessments and conversations, and connect with the right services. We are not nurses, doctors, social workers, lawyers or financial advisors. We don't assess care needs, determine eligibility or recommend a level of care. Eligibility for publicly funded home care and long-term care is decided by Ontario Health atHome, and decisions rest with your parent or their substitute decision-maker.

When something needs a professional, such as a lawyer for a power of attorney or an accountant for tax questions, we'll say so and note it as an action in your plan.
